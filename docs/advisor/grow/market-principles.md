# Grow customer and offer evidence

TOPIC: grow.market-principles
PURPOSE: Ground a growth plan in a testable customer/problem/offer hypothesis.
WHEN TO USE: Audience selection, positioning, market research, campaign briefs or unclear demand.
REQUIRED FACTS: Target role/company or audience; observed pain and source; desired outcome; buying/decision authority where relevant; reachable channel; offer and evidence; constraints.

STABLE PRINCIPLES
A customer profile and offer are hypotheses until supported by relevant observations. Name whose problem is solved, what changes for them and why the claim is credible. Distinguish user need from the owner's preference. A source-backed statement still needs scope, authenticity and relevance; traceability alone is not truth. MISSING != ZERO and a competitor's public activity does not establish profitability.

DECISION TREE
What outcome does the user want? Which audience has the evidenced problem? Can the business reach and serve that audience with the available offer/assets/capacity? What small observation or manual test would challenge the hypothesis? If the evidence is thin, prepare a focused question or comparison before choosing a large channel plan. Explicitly state what evidence would change the recommendation.

For a local Grow brief, keep observed facts, owner claims and proposals distinct. Use owned product information and assets, retaining provenance and rights. Current source review and actual project status outrank a prior plan. Advisor's ordinary state projection does not contain full customer interviews, budgets or market reports: ask for decision-changing missing facts rather than inferring them from a DRAFT/CURRENT label.

Public competitor research can identify positioning, format and claim hypotheses from appropriately accessible public material. It cannot reveal unseen targeting, spend, conversion rates, market share or business economics. Compare like offers and audiences; an outlier may reflect distribution, timing or an existing audience rather than a repeatable creative tactic.

COMMON FAILURE MODES
Starting with a platform instead of a goal; treating an invented persona as research; copying a competitor claim; collecting unnecessary personal data; using public views as revenue proof; building many assets before establishing one coherent offer.

STOP CONDITIONS: Unsubstantiated claim, unknown ownership/rights, private data request, unclear customer outcome or a plan exceeding capacity. Stop the affected recommendation; retain useful stable guidance. No scraping system, purchased contact list or autonomous outreach belongs to this card.
WHAT NOT TO CLAIM: Guaranteed leads/revenue, optimal market, public-data completeness or permission to contact/spend.
NEXT ACTION / OUTPUT SHAPE: Customer/offer hypothesis; evidence and alternatives; smallest test; acceptance/stop condition; evidence that would change the plan.
SOURCE STATUS: Distribution guidance. Owner source paths are indexed in knowledge.mjs; internal maintenance provenance is not distributed or verified by the public suite.
