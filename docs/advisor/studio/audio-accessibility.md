# Present audio and accessibility

TOPIC: studio.audio-accessibility
PURPOSE: Keep speech understandable and essential information available without inventing audio controls.
WHEN TO USE: Music/noise, cut speech, silence review, captions/description, flashing or motion concerns.
REQUIRED FACTS: Actual listening/viewing observations; source audio; cut positions; required information; intended audience/player; available editing controls.

STABLE PRINCIPLES
Speech should remain intelligible over competing sound. Allow time to process the demonstrated action and result. Captions communicate meaningful audio; description or suitable alternatives communicate essential visual information. Neither exported captions nor a single frame establishes accessibility of the complete viewing experience.

DECISION TREE
Listen at ordinary volume on headphones and a phone speaker. Locate masked words, clipping/crackle, noise, abrupt level changes and cuts through speech. Preserve pauses that support meaning. If the source is unclear, recommend a quieter recording or clearly external preprocessing, followed by reviewed video import. Advisor does not execute that workflow.

Present carries source audio through retained cuts and supports bounded source audition/review. It has no music/narration track mixer, source-audio replacement, gain, ducking, EQ, denoise, limiter or loudness-meter controls. Do not recommend clicking a nonexistent mixing control. Loudness and true peak are different measurements; a normalization label does not prove undistorted sound. There is no universal social-platform loudness target established here.

For accessibility, check accurate captions, important sounds/speaker labels where needed, visible text contrast and explanation of critical visual outcomes. “The status changed to Ready” communicates more than “look here.” A descriptive transcript can help but does not automatically satisfy every applicable requirement. Review the final player, controls and alternatives as well as the video.

Avoid unnecessary rapid pans, zoom pulses and flashes. A rendered video does not automatically follow the viewer's reduced-motion setting. Check motion/flashing concerns under the relevant standard rather than treating a count of cuts as a safety certificate. Silence suggestions are review candidates, not permission to remove meaningful footage.

COMMON FAILURE MODES
Universal loudness rules, music masking speech, an automatic transcript called accurate, silent visuals mistaken for disposable footage, or media generation treated as required setup.

STOP CONDITIONS: Missing listening evidence, inaccessible essential information, distorted source or unsupported controls. Preserve the original; request a bounded observation or rerecording. ElevenLabs is not required competition functionality and is not a prerequisite for local audio/caption work.
WHAT NOT TO CLAIM: Hearing the recording without audio, compliance certification, restored speech quality or provider qualification.
